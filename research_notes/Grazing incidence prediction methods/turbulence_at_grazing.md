## 1. Effect of turbulence on the energy-mean level, flush and 1.2 m receivers, lit and shadow regions

### Takeaway
In a lit region a flush receiver's energy-mean level is essentially unchanged by turbulence (energy is conserved, backscatter loss is ~1e-3 dB or less), and a 1.2 m receiver's level changes only through loss of direct/reflected coherence, which scales as k² and so cannot be flat across 63–630 Hz. In refractive shadows the picture reverses: small-scale (Kolmogorov) scattering gives shadow levels that are *weakly* frequency dependent above a few hundred hertz, and large-scale (energy-containing) fluctuations displace rays by a frequency-independent amount, so near a shadow boundary turbulence can raise the energy mean by a few dB roughly uniformly in frequency.

### Cited Findings
**Lit region, energy conservation and coherence**
- Clifford & Lataitis give an "energy conserving" treatment of the mean-square pressure above a boundary, with "an arbitrary refractive-index correlation function". **[EARLIER NOTE, ABS]** — [Clifford & Lataitis, JASA 73, 1545 (1983)](https://doi.org/10.1121/1.389416)
- The mean-square pressure over ground contains a coherence factor equal to "the normalized coherence function of a spherical sound wave for line-of-sight propagation", with closed forms for Kolmogorov, Gaussian and von Kármán spectra. **[EARLIER NOTE, ABS]** — [Ostashev et al., JASA 109, 1894 (2001)](https://doi.org/10.1121/1.1356022)
- The 2024 extension covers backscatter attenuation of both waves, height-dependent variances and length scales, and slanted paths from elevated sources. "Numerical results for slanted propagation demonstrate that atmospheric turbulence can significantly increase the sound pressure level at the interference minima." **[ABS]** — [Ostashev, Breton & Wilson, JASA 156, 107 (2024)](https://pubs.aip.org/asa/jasa/article/156/1/107/3302105/Interference-of-the-direct-and-ground-reflected)
- The theory is general (Markov approximation, PE moments) for "line-of-sight propagation, multipath propagation in a refractive atmosphere above an impedance ground, and sound scattering into a refractive shadow zone". **[EARLIER NOTE, ABS]** — [Ostashev, Wilson, Vecherin & Collier, JASA 136, 2414 (2014)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25373944/)
- The total scattering cross-section (the extinction rate of the *coherent* field) for a modified von Kármán spectrum is σ0 = (3/5) π A k² L0 (T'²/4T0² + v'²/c0²) (their Eq. 11): it grows as k². The differential cross-section (their Eq. 8) carries a k^(1/3) factor times [sin²(θ/2) + (2kL0)^-2]^(-11/6), with a cos²θ factor and a velocity term proportional to sin²θ/[…]. **[FULL]** — [Cotté & Blanc-Benon, Acta Acust. united Acust. 93 (2007)](https://perso.ensta-paris.fr/~cotte/files/Cotte_BlancBenon_acta93_2007.pdf)
- Downwind, turbulence effects on the received spectrum "are rather small". **[EARLIER NOTE, ABS]** — [Salomons 1994](https://doi.org/10.1121/1.409852)
- Elevated source (wind turbine), PE with and without turbulence: "atmospheric turbulence has a negligible effect on SPL up to 800 m from the wind turbine, even under upwind conditions". **[EARLIER NOTE, ABS]** — [Kayser, Écotière & Gauvreau, Acta Acustica 7, 63 (2023)](https://doi.org/10.1051/aacus/2023051)
- Measurements and modeling for an elevated source: "turbulence effects are smaller for elevated sources than for sources close to the ground"; the paper built a height model of the variance and scale of index fluctuations from measurements. **[SNIPPET]** — [Johnson, Raspet & Bobak, JASA (1987), "A turbulence model for sound propagation from an elevated source above level ground"](https://labs.sonicfield.org/library/a-turbulence-model-for-sound-propagation-from-an-elevated-source-above-level-gro)
- Laboratory N-waves into a shadow over a curved surface through thermal turbulence: in the lit zone "turbulence attenuates the peak pressure by a factor of approximately 0.8, with random amplification or attenuation"; in the shadow "a systematic amplification of the peak pressure by a factor of up to 8"; "a doubling of the pressure level is a common event, with a probability of about 70%"; the shadow is "dominated by pressure waves previously scattered from the illuminated zone". (These are peak-pressure statistics of a transient, not energy means.) **[ABS]** — [Salze et al., AIAA J. (2025)](https://consensus.app/papers/details/eca3763b9bc25804a62a66ba6308f29e/?utm_source=claude_desktop)

**Shadow region: scattering, its frequency dependence, and the scales involved**
- Wideband tones 40–940 Hz in a refractive shadow: "at 40 and 90 Hz, the effect of turbulence was negligible. At 210 Hz and above, turbulence effects were significant. From 380 to 940 Hz, the shadow zone levels showed a relatively weak dependence on frequency." A Gaussian spectrum "gave a strong frequency dependence"; the Kolmogorov model agreed "over the entire band". **[ABS]** — [Di, Gilbert, et al. (1995)](https://consensus.app/papers/details/fe8b084dd4415b7ea95ebe41ab2217bb/?utm_source=claude_desktop)
- Distorted-wave Born analysis explains why: measurements (Havelock et al.) show "above a few hundred Hertz, the sound levels in a refractive shadow zone are weakly dependent on frequency", consistent with scattering from a Kolmogorov spectrum; a "sampling function" selects the contributing part of the spectrum. **[ABS]** — [Gilbert et al., JASA (1996)](https://consensus.app/papers/details/691dead398905913b3139ff366ee7368/?utm_source=claude_desktop)
- PE with turbulence against Wiener & Keast: a "step" in level versus range; in the shadow "weak dependence on range, height, and frequency"; "for a receiver deep in a shadow zone and frequencies greater than a few hundred hertz, the measured sound-pressure level is due almost entirely to sound scattered into the shadow zone". **[EARLIER NOTE, ABS]** — [Gilbert, Raspet & Di, JASA 87, 2428 (1990)](https://pubs.aip.org/asa/jasa/article-abstract/87/6/2428/893181/Calculation-of-turbulence-effects-in-an-upward?redirectedFrom=fulltext)
- Single-scatter simulation against Daigle's 230 m shadow data (measured ⟨μ²⟩ = 6e-6, L = 1.6 m): scattering and diffraction "about equal at 250 Hz", scattering "the major contributor at 500 Hz", "predominant" at 1 kHz. **[EARLIER NOTE, FULL]** — [McBride et al., NASA](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/19910007390/downloads/19910007390.pdf?attachment=true)
- For a 500 Hz tone in an upward-refracting log profile to 1 km, the turbulence scales that "contributed most to the scattered field in the acoustic shadow region corresponded to dimensions of the order of 2–5 m", understood by a Bragg condition. **[ABS]** — [Stinson (1994)](https://consensus.app/papers/details/b0f39806e98a5faf89121d6fcd36ac55/?utm_source=claude_desktop)
- PE, third-octave bands 50–1600 Hz, to 1 km: the smallest relevant scales follow Bragg, K_Cmax = 2k = 4πf/c0 (linear in frequency); the largest follow the Fresnel radius, valid while scattering is unsaturated. At 400 Hz, a 1 m receiver: turbulence effects appear beyond ~150 m "corresponding to the shadow zone. At these ranges the relative sound pressure level remains relatively constant"; truncating the spectrum at K_max ≤ 4 m^-1 leaves the shadow level short, and a K_max = 2 m^-1 run was "10 dB lower than the reference level at a height of 1 m, but within 1 dB … at a height of 20 m". About 30 realizations suffice for the mean; with 25 vs 30, velocity-turbulence results differ by up to 2 dB at 1600 Hz and 25 m. **[FULL]** — [Cotté & Blanc-Benon 2007](https://perso.ensta-paris.fr/~cotte/files/Cotte_BlancBenon_acta93_2007.pdf)
- Scattering strength "depends not only on turbulence parameters but also on acoustic frequency and geometry (range, source and receiver heights)"; source and receiver heights limit the eddy sizes that contribute. **[ABS]** — [Cotté & Blanc-Benon, Inter-Noise 2006](https://researchportal.ip-paris.fr/en/publications/coupling-between-turbulence-scales-acoustic-frequency-and-geometr/)
- With flux–profile similarity supplying both refraction and turbulence, shadow levels "decrease with the adverse wind … Conversely, they increase with the absolute value of the surface heat flux". **[ABS]** — [Cheinet, JASA 131, 1946 (2012)](https://consensus.app/papers/details/68a060f31379591d91cc9afddb588939/?utm_source=claude_desktop)
- Turbulent-scattered-ray model: rays are diffracted from the shadow boundary, then displaced by turbulence; the cumulative height change of a ray, Δz_{i+1} ≈ φ0 − √(μ0²) Δx² Σ q_j − …, "is independent of frequency", so one ray-tracing run for one strength scales to others. With μ0² = 3e-5 ("considered to be the upper limit of turbulence that one would normally encounter") the shadow attenuation "is severely limited, reaching just over −10 to −20 dB at 10 Hz, and around −20 dB at the higher frequencies even at 10 km into the shadow". Against White & Gilbert's upwind data (40 and 630 Hz, gradients −0.034 and −0.068 s^-1, μ0² = 1e-6 and 2e-6) it lies within the measured min–max. **[FULL]** — [Lam, JASA 125, 1340 (2009)](https://salford-repository.worktribe.com/OutputFile/1499569)

**1.2 m receiver: where the interference minima are at 2–6°**
- The 1.2 m pole's measured transfer near grazing "rests on the pole's modeled ground reflection (−10 to −14 dB at 125–500 Hz below 2°)". **[PROJECT]** — `research_notes/Grazing incidence soft ground propagation/current_model_and_residuals.md`, row 5 of the residual table.

### Inferences
- **[OWN CALC]** *Backscatter extinction is negligible.* With the sodar backscatter cross-section η(π) = 0.0039 k^(1/3) C_T²/T0² m^-1 sr^-1 **[RECALLED]** (velocity does not backscatter at exactly 180°), a crude 2π-sr hemisphere over 4 km gives a fractional energy loss of 1e-6 (C_T² = 1e-3 K² m^-2/3, 63 Hz) to 5e-4 (C_T² = 0.1, 5 kHz): at most ~2e-3 dB. Ostashev 2024's backscatter term cannot be a dB-level effect at these ranges.
- **[RECALLED]** For line-of-sight propagation in the Markov approximation the mean intensity ⟨I⟩ is unchanged by turbulence; only the coherent field ⟨p⟩ (extinction ∝ k², Cotté Eq. 11) and the fluctuations change. Combined with energy conservation (Clifford–Lataitis) and the point above, a **flush receiver in a lit region gets no energy-mean change from turbulence**, flat or otherwise. The exception is a lit receiver near a caustic or the shadow boundary, where fluctuations redistribute energy along the boundary (see below).
- **[OWN CALC]** *1.2 m pole at 2–6°.* Path difference 2 h_r sin ψ = 0.084, 0.167, 0.251, 0.417 m at ψ = 2, 4, 6, 10°, so the first rigid-ground dip sits at 2030, 1015, 678, 408 Hz. Across 63–630 Hz, therefore, a 1.2 m microphone at 2–6° is mostly on the constructive side; only the soft-ground phase (the −10 to −14 dB modeled dip below 2°) puts minima in band. Using HARMONOISE's Γ = exp[−(3/8)(0.364) k² γ_p^(5/3) R γ_T] **[RECALLED]** with γ_p ≈ h_r = 1.2 m and a constructive interference with |Q| = 0.6, the level change is:

  | R, γ_T | 63 Hz | 125 | 250 | 500 | 630 | 1 k | 2 k |
  | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
  | 1 km, 3e-6 | 0.0 | 0.0 | 0.0 | −0.1 | −0.2 | −0.4 | −1.2 |
  | 1 km, 3e-5 | 0.0 | −0.1 | −0.2 | −0.8 | −1.2 | −2.2 | −2.7 |
  | 4 km, 3e-6 | 0.0 | 0.0 | −0.1 | −0.4 | −0.6 | −1.2 | −2.6 |
  | 4 km, 3e-5 | −0.1 | −0.2 | −0.8 | −2.2 | −2.6 | −2.7 | −2.7 |

  The sign is negative (turbulence *lowers* a constructive-interference maximum toward the incoherent sum) and the dependence is steep in frequency. At a dip the sign reverses and the change can be many dB, also frequency-selective. Neither looks like a flat 1.5–4 dB offset over 63–630 Hz, and none of it applies to flush plates (γ_p = 0).
- The two shadow mechanisms differ in spectral signature. *Small-scale scattering*: the Kolmogorov differential cross-section gives k^(1/3), +1.0 dB per octave, +3.3 dB over 63→630 Hz **[OWN CALC]**. The field data are flatter than that above ~300 Hz (Di 1995; Gilbert 1996), and NICEOPS's own PE-fitted `ShadowScattering` for a flush microphone has b = −1.55 dB per decade **[PROJECT]**: across 63–630 Hz it is flat to 1.6 dB. Below ~200 Hz scattering is negligible against diffraction (Di 1995). *Large-scale eddies* act as random refraction; in the ray limit the displacement is exactly frequency-independent (Lam's Eq. 19).
- So a regime where turbulence raises the energy-mean level by 1–3 dB roughly uniformly over 63–630 Hz plausibly exists: a receiver in the **penumbra just inside a mean shadow boundary**, where the boundary wanders by more than the diffraction width. There the energy mean over boundary positions exceeds the level at the mean position, by an amount set by the geometric wander, not by k. This is an inference from Lam's frequency-independent displacement and Wilson et al.'s attribution of upwind errors to "the variable position of the shadow-zone boundary" (section 3). I found no paper that quantifies it for 63–630 Hz bands at 2–6°.
- The opposite regime, a roughly flat *lowering*, would come from turbulence smearing a caustic. Under nonlinear upwind profiles, elevated sources get local SPL increases "of up to 7 dB" from caustic focusing at intermediate ranges before the shadow ([Gharbi et al., Acta Acustica (2026)](https://consensus.app/papers/details/89f6fa0ca10555559ddad6abe66d18f4/?utm_source=claude_desktop) **[ABS]**). Random refraction would lower the mean peak and spread it. I found no quantification. NICEOPS already caps focusing at +10 dB **[PROJECT]**.
- Where NICEOPS sums multiple arrivals *in energy* (stratified ducts) **[PROJECT]**, turbulence-driven decorrelation between arrivals changes nothing in the mean. That removes another candidate lit-region mechanism.

### Gaps
- Ostashev, Breton & Wilson (2024): the magnitude of the minimum-filling for slanted paths, and their height-dependent coherence formula, are in the body only.
- No source quantifies the energy-mean gain from shadow-boundary wander (penumbra) as a function of frequency band and boundary-position variance. This is the mechanism most able to produce a flat offset, and it is the least documented.
- Kayser et al. (2023): the "simple formulation" for turbulence-induced uncertainty and the frequency breakdown were inaccessible.
- Havelock et al.'s measured shadow levels versus frequency (the data behind Gilbert 1996) were not found.

## 2. Slanted-path turbulence from elevated sources, and estimating C_T², C_v² from meteorology

### Takeaway
Slant paths differ from horizontal ones because turbulence strength falls with height (roughly z^(-2/3) for shear turbulence) and because the ground blocks large vertical eddies. Measured log-amplitude variance and spatial coherence on 0–130 m slant paths agree with height-dependent shear-plus-buoyancy models in developed turbulence, but phase variance is overpredicted, and dawn and night cases are poorly predicted. At 2–6° elevation a path still spends 0.5–1.4 km in the lowest 50 m, so for this brief it is closer to horizontal than to vertical. Surface-layer similarity makes γ_T vary by more than an order of magnitude between 1 m and 150 m. That can reconcile a pole-pair fit of 3e-5 (set by the lowest meters) with a shadow and SEL fit of ≤3e-6 (set by the air tens of meters up, often in a weak morning boundary layer).

### Cited Findings
- 2018 NWTC experiment: ground loudspeaker, nine microphones on booms of a 135 m tower (39, 80, 130 m), vertical and slanted (source 56 m from the tower base) paths. "The theory correctly predicts the measured log-amplitude variances, which are affected primarily by small-scale, isotropic turbulent eddies. However, the theory overpredicts the measured phase variances, which are affected primarily by large-scale, anisotropic, buoyantly driven eddies. Ground blocking of these large eddies likely explains the overprediction." **[ABS]** — [Kamrath et al., JASA 149, 2055 (2021)](https://consensus.app/papers/details/4150b23eaf625049a7f3a3183e2d1cbb/?utm_source=claude_desktop)
- Spatial coherence "primarily affected by small-scale isotropic turbulence" is predicted correctly; cross-frequency coherence and long-time temporal coherence, "affected by large-scale anisotropic buoyancy-driven velocity fluctuations", are underpredicted; distributions agree "for different regimes ranging from unsaturated to fully saturated scattering". **[ABS]** — [Ostashev et al., JASA 150, 3109 (2021)](https://consensus.app/papers/details/34006174cb3650d181807602f2cc1eed/?utm_source=claude_desktop)
- Ground blocking: "since the sound waves are impacted most strongly by the turbulent velocity fluctuations in the direction of propagation, and the ground blocks large vertical motions, the turbulence impacts are relatively weakened for vertical paths"; a way to include it in the spectrum is described. **[ABS, meeting]** — [Wilson et al. (2022)](https://consensus.app/papers/details/d3b1382349e25912b960d445d5a3c5d0/?utm_source=claude_desktop)
- Measured spatial coherence at 3.4 kHz, microphones 130 m up and 1.5 m apart: 0.3–0.6 on sunny days, 0.8–0.9 when cloudy; "the largest disagreement occurred for measurements taken at dawn". **[ABS, meeting]** — [Kamrath et al. (2022)](https://consensus.app/papers/details/696f254b24045cfa86148f83957b5c4a/?utm_source=claude_desktop)
- Temporal coherence, 0.6–3.5 kHz, 39–130 m: coherence times 0.1–100 s, falling "slightly more than an order of magnitude" from lowest to highest frequency and slightly less than an order of magnitude from shortest to longest path; "in the two early-morning trials, the measured coherence times were much larger than predicted because the atmospheric turbulence was not fully developed". **[ABS, meeting]** — [Kamrath et al. (2021)](https://consensus.app/papers/details/f1abe7daa77458de8052dcdecda0a32d/?utm_source=claude_desktop)
- The dependence of the statistics on meteorology "was found to be accurately predicted in conditions of well-developed turbulence, as occurs in windy conditions and the daytime. Predictions in conditions of weak and intermittent turbulence, as often occur at night and around sunrise and sunset, remain challenging." **[ABS, meeting]** — [Wilson et al. (2024)](https://consensus.app/papers/details/e245fcc973c0590b9f6e9e7575095464/?utm_source=claude_desktop)
- Height-dependent von Kármán inputs used for slant paths (unstable/neutral forms): σ_T² = 4T*²/(1 − 10z/L_o)^(2/3), σ²_v,s = 3u*², σ²_v,b = 0.35 w*², L_T = 2z(1 − 7z/L_o)/(1 − 10z/L_o), L_v,s = 1.8z, L_v,b = 0.23 z_i, T* = −Q_H/(ρ0 c_p u*), w* = (z_i g Q_H/ρ0 c_p T0)^(1/3). The test cases used u* = 0.1 or 0.5 m/s, Q_H = 50 or 200 W/m², z_i = 500 or 2000 m. "The temperature fluctuations have a negligible influence" on the coherence; low wind → buoyancy-produced velocity dominates; strong wind → shear-produced. **[FULL]** — [Lincke et al. 2023](https://elib.dlr.de/194540/2/10.0016847.pdf)
- A physics-based scintillation model for slanted and vertical paths (aircraft, drones, wind turbines) uses spherical-wave log-amplitude and phase correlation functions, the von Kármán spectrum and similarity theory. **[ABS]** — [Bresciani et al., JASA (2023)](https://consensus.app/papers/details/b63c1f1f18385147a5c2f82d52c6c5c0/?utm_source=claude_desktop)
- Stable surface-layer MOST function for C_T² from CASES-99 eddy-correlation data: f_T = 4.7[1 + 1.6 (z/L)^(2/3)]. A multi-dataset fit covering unstable and stable conditions also exists. **[SNIPPET]** — [Surface-layer similarity functions for dissipation rate and structure parameters (RUG/WUR record)](https://research.rug.nl/en/publications/surface-layer-similarity-functions-for-dissipation-rate-and-struc/)
- Wilson et al.'s four regimes: high wind (neutral, refraction by direction); low-wind clear day (unstable, upward refraction everywhere, "buoyancy instabilities create strong turbulence"); low-wind cloudy ("turbulence tends to be weak"); low-wind clear night (stable). Representative PE inputs: u* = 0.6 m/s, Q_H = 0; u* = 0.1, Q_H = 200 W/m²; u* = 0; u* = 0.08, Q_H = 0 to −10 W/m². "Because stable stratification suppresses turbulence, conventional turbulence similarity theories such as MOST no longer apply." **[FULL]** — [Wilson, Pettit & Ostashev, Acoustics Today 11(2), 44 (2015)](https://acousticstoday.org/wp-content/uploads/2015/06/Sound-Propagation-in-the-Atmospheric-Boundary-Layer-D.-Keith-Wilson-Chris-L.-Pettit-and-Vladimir-E.-Ostashev.pdf)
- Doppler lidar can retrieve the dissipation rate ε: from the variance of sequential mean Doppler velocities with noise removed, over "at least three orders of magnitude" against a balloon sonic ([O'Connor et al. 2010](https://consensus.app/papers/details/7f3220f6a20859ada1ae2ddeffeba4cd/?utm_source=claude_desktop)); from VAD/conical scans via azimuth structure functions, where "lidar measurements can be significantly improved by the introduction of the volume-averaging effect" and low-turbulence regimes are underestimated ([Wildmann et al. 2020](https://consensus.app/papers/details/d4867ae7dbcd5305bf62372e349e06d9/?utm_source=claude_desktop)); neglecting probe-volume averaging "leads to the understating the dissipation rate" ([Banakh et al. 2021](https://consensus.app/papers/details/cf5b3bf7f03e549fb188b79cda370598/?utm_source=claude_desktop)); and ε by stability, height and wind speed from four lidars against sonics ([Bodini et al. 2018](https://consensus.app/papers/details/f69bd9f0e15859979702cada1773e351/?utm_source=claude_desktop)). **[ABS]**

- **Reconciling NICEOPS's two γ_T values.** The 3e-5 from the pole/board pair fit is set by decorrelation within meters of a 1.2 m microphone, where daytime γ_T is 2e-5 to 7e-5 in the table above. The ≤3e-6 that the SEL supports is set by scattering at the heights of the scattering volume (tens of meters, source to ground), in a morning that was probably weakly turbulent. Both can be right at once. A single uniform γ_T cannot be. The structural fix is a height-dependent C²(z) in the PE screens and in the coherence factor (Ostashev 2024; Lincke's inputs), not a compromise constant. NICEOPS's PE uses "uniform in height" screens and a 10 m outer scale **[PROJECT]**; the near-ground outer scale should be ~2z (L_T = 2z, L_v,s = 1.8z).
- **From the 2017 instrumentation.** The ZephIR lidar's lowest gate is 46 ft (14 m) **[PROJECT]**. A lidar ε at 14–150 m gives C_v²(z) = 2.0 ε^(2/3) directly; taking u* from the lowest gate needs a φ_ε assumption and the stability (from the balloon temperature gradient via a bulk Richardson number). Lidar ε is biased low in weak turbulence and by volume averaging (Wildmann 2020; Banakh 2021), which is the morning regime of the flights. C_T² below 14 m has to come from MOST with T* from the balloon string's 0–10 ft gradient, and that is the poorest-known input.
- Ground-blocking anisotropy (Wilson 2022) weakens the large-scale *vertical* velocity effect that dominates vertical paths. For near-horizontal paths the along-path (horizontal) velocity matters, so the isotropic models are less wrong at 2–6°.

### Gaps
- Stable-boundary-layer formulas for σ², L and the spectral form used by Ostashev & Wilson (Wilson 2000; their book, ch. 6–7): inaccessible. The night rows above use generic MOST functions, not the acoustic-community model.
- No measured slant-path acoustic statistics at 0–10° elevation from elevated sources (the NWTC geometry is steeper; UAV campaigns found only as abstracts).
- No source gives a validated conversion from Doppler-lidar turbulence intensity (ZephIR 1 s σ_u) to C_v² near the ground; the route through ε is standard meteorology, but its accuracy in weak morning turbulence is reported as poor.
- The measured u*, Q_H and ε for the 2017 flight days are not in any file I read **[PROJECT]**; the table is indicative only.

## 3. Intermittency and level-fluctuation statistics; comparing a deterministic prediction with short-frame energy means

### Takeaway
Single-frequency level fluctuations saturate at high frequency and long range (σ ≈ 5.6 dB for fully developed, Rayleigh-like intensity), and are roughly Gaussian in dB (log-normal intensity) before that. A gamma distribution of power spans the two regimes. A mean-profile deterministic prediction targets the long-time energy mean, and does so with <2 dB rms outside shadows and minima. A 0.5 s third-octave σ of 2–4 dB is consistent with a saturated field averaged over only ~2–5 independent frequency cells. The energy mean of a few correlated frames scatters by ±1–3 dB but is biased *low*. A positive (measured-louder), roughly frequency-flat bias can come from frame selection: dropping frames below a noise threshold set on a broadband level.

### Cited Findings
- Level fluctuations "have an approximately gaussian distribution" in dB after propagation through turbulence. In optics the intensity fluctuation saturates for 2σ_l greater than ~3 "at a value of about 1.4"; acoustic saturation "occurs … at the level predicted by theory". Ottawa summer, low wind, neutral or lapse, 50–1000 ft, 150–5000 Hz: "at low frequencies or shorter distances the fluctuations are not saturated, at high frequencies and larger distances the fluctuations are always saturated", with a weather-dependent transition. **[FULL]** — [Embleton, Piercy & Olson, "Sound propagation outdoors", Canadian Acoustics (NRC)](https://jcaa.caa-aca.ca/index.php/jcaa/article/download/386/47/437)
- Line-of-sight tones 250–4000 Hz, 2–300 m: log-amplitude fluctuations are "substantially smaller than predicted and … show clear evidence of saturation"; coherences are then "essentially a function of the phase variance only". **[EARLIER NOTE, ABS]** — [Daigle, Piercy & Embleton, JASA 74, 1505 (1983)](https://doi.org/10.1121/1.390152)
- Saturation boundaries for a von Kármán spectrum (L0 = 5 m, μ² = 1e-5): f_lim = (c0/2πL_T)(2μ²)^(-1/3) = 534 Hz, r_sat(f_lim) = 138 m; below f_lim, r_sat = 1/(2 L_T k² μ²); above it, r_sat = L_T^0.8/(2^1.2 k^1.4 μ^2.4)^(1/2.2) **[as extracted; check the exponent]**. "For large ranges and high frequencies, the simulations correspond to saturated scattering conditions." **[FULL]** — [Cotté & Blanc-Benon 2007](https://perso.ensta-paris.fr/~cotte/files/Cotte_BlancBenon_acta93_2007.pdf)
- A gamma distribution of power with a wrapped-normal phase fits NWTC slant-path data "across a wide range of scattering regimes"; the gamma "approximates a log-normal distribution when the normalized power variance is small compared to one and an exponential distribution when the normalized power variance is one". **[ABS, meeting]** — [Kamrath (2022)](https://consensus.app/papers/details/3867aeaf20185e029d36bfc7f524e1ec/?utm_source=claude_desktop)
- CASES-99 at 150 Hz, 570–1170 m: "deep fading episodes exceeding 20 dB are present … sound levels can change by this amount in a matter of minutes"; timing and amplitude are not predicted "in a deterministic sense". From LES-based studies: (1) "mean vertical profiles produce the most accurate predictions of both mean and event sound levels"; (2) mean-level predictions from mean profiles have "rms errors, typically less than 2 dB, except in shadow zones and interference minima, where scattering by turbulence is important"; (3) averaging calculations from many instantaneous profiles "results in overprediction of sound levels in upward refraction due to the plywood atmosphere effect"; (4) event predictions have "rms errors of 8-10 dB". Means need "roughly 30 min or longer" of meteorological averaging. In an uncertainty example, upwind errors were "about twice as large" as downwind, "likely due to the variable position of the shadow-zone boundary". **[FULL]** — [Wilson, Pettit & Ostashev 2015](https://acousticstoday.org/wp-content/uploads/2015/06/Sound-Propagation-in-the-Atmospheric-Boundary-Layer-D.-Keith-Wilson-Chris-L.-Pettit-and-Vladimir-E.-Ostashev.pdf)
- Ingard (1953) recorded random variations of 10–20 dB, attributed to wind gustiness. **[FULL, as quoted by]** — [Wilson et al. 2015](https://acousticstoday.org/wp-content/uploads/2015/06/Sound-Propagation-in-the-Atmospheric-Boundary-Layer-D.-Keith-Wilson-Chris-L.-Pettit-and-Vladimir-E.-Ostashev.pdf)
- In shadow zones, level variations "might be as large as 20 dB" depending on whether the boundary layer is strongly or weakly scattering; engineering models' "distance independent constant value … relative to free field" misses this. **[ABS]** — [Van Renterghem et al., Inter-Noise 2023](https://consensus.app/papers/details/22efe341dcaf52d8a779d184086712c6/?utm_source=claude_desktop)
- A single turbulence realization agreed better with an 8 km experiment than the 64-realization average TL. **[EARLIER NOTE, ABS]** — Hart, Wilson, Pettit & Nykaza (cited in `numerical_methods_grazing.md`)
- Temporal coherence times of 0.1–100 s on 40–130 m paths (Kamrath 2021, above).

### Inferences
- **[OWN CALC]** Fully saturated (exponential) intensity: σ_L = 5.57 dB, and the dB-mean lies 2.51 dB below the energy mean. Log-normal frames: energy mean minus dB mean = σ² ln10/20 = 0.46, 1.04, 1.84 dB for σ = 2, 3, 4 dB.
- **[OWN CALC]** If a band level is a sum of M independent exponential components (a band wider than the cross-frequency coherence bandwidth), σ_L = 5.57, 3.49, 2.73, 2.04, 1.41 dB for M = 1, 2, 3, 5, 10. The measured σ = 2–4 dB for 0.5 s third-octave frames is consistent with saturation and M ≈ 2–5, or with partial saturation. Rotor source unsteadiness adds to it, so σ alone cannot measure turbulence strength.
- **[OWN CALC]** Sampling of 10 lg(energy mean of N *independent* log-normal frames), relative to the true energy mean (bias; 5–95%):

  | σ (dB) | N = 3 | N = 5 | N = 10 | N = 20 | N = 60 |
  | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
  | 2 | −0.16; [−2.1, +1.8] | −0.10; [−1.6, +1.4] | −0.05; [−1.1, +1.1] | −0.03; [−0.8, +0.8] | −0.01; [−0.5, +0.4] |
  | 3 | −0.39; [−3.4, +2.7] | −0.25; [−2.6, +2.2] | −0.12; [−1.8, +1.6] | −0.06; [−1.3, +1.2] | −0.02; [−0.7, +0.7] |
  | 4 | −0.75; [−4.8, +3.5] | −0.49; [−3.7, +2.9] | −0.26; [−2.6, +2.2] | −0.13; [−1.9, +1.7] | −0.05; [−1.1, +1.0] |

  Frames 0.5 s apart are correlated wherever the coherence time exceeds 0.5 s (Kamrath: 0.1–100 s), so the effective N is smaller than the frame count. The bias of a few-frame energy mean is **negative** (Jensen). It cannot explain prediction being too quiet, and it does not repeat in sign across microphone lines.
- **[OWN CALC]** *Censoring bias.* If frames are kept only when above a threshold (signal-to-noise, or a level relative to the event peak) and the energy mean is taken over the kept frames, the bias is +0.4–0.6 dB (threshold at mean − σ, 84% kept), +1.3–2.2 dB (at the mean, 50% kept) and +2.7–4.7 dB (mean + σ, 16% kept) for σ = 2–4 dB. If the selection is made once, on a broadband or A-weighted level, every band inherits the same frame set and so nearly the same bias. The result would be a **roughly frequency-flat, positive, range-increasing** measured-minus-predicted offset, largest exactly where SNR is lowest: far ahead, near the horizon. This is a hypothesis to test in the harness (compare residuals with and without frame selection, or against SNR); I have not checked how the frames are selected.
- Wilson's result (3) has a bearing on how profiles are fed in. If NICEOPS were run per frame on instantaneous lidar profiles and averaged, upward-refraction shadows would be over-filled (predicted too loud). Running on block-mean profiles, which Wilson et al. recommend, does not have that bias but misses shadow-boundary wander (section 1).

### Gaps
- No published measured σ_L for band-limited aircraft or helicopter noise at 0–10° and 1–4 km; the tone statistics above are the closest.
- The cross-frequency coherence bandwidth at these ranges (needed for M) is underpredicted by the theory (Ostashev 2021) and was not found as a number.

## 4. How engineering and aircraft models treat turbulence near grazing; calibrated γ_T and C² values

### Takeaway
Only the Nordic and European "comprehensive" methods carry turbulence explicitly: Nord2000 (C_v², C_T², plus a gradient-fluctuation input) and Harmonoise/Imagine (one γ_T for both coherence loss and shadow scattering, typical 5e-6, sometimes 1e-5). ISO 9613-2 and the aircraft tools (AEDT/INM, the FAA's Nord2000 trial apart) include it only implicitly, through empirical caps and empirical lateral attenuation. No model found here maps γ_T to stability classes in a validated way. The recommended defaults correspond to near-neutral, moderately windy surface layers.

### Cited Findings
- Nord2000 inputs: C_v² "accounts for the turbulent motion of the atmosphere", recommended 0.12 m^(4/3) s^-2 "unless other information is available", maximum observed ~0.3; C_T² "accounts for the turbulent variation of the temperature", recommended 0.008 (the page gives the unit as "K/s²", evidently a slip for K² m^(-2/3)), maximum ~0.05; a "standard deviation of temperature gradient fluctuations" (°C/m), recommended 0, for "fluctuation in temperature gradient in excess of what is already included in the turbulence strength"; a stability-class option is "not available for the time being". **[FULL, vendor documentation]** — [DataKustik CadnaA manual, "Industrie Nord2000"](https://doku.datakustik.com/CadnaA/en/Industrie_Nord2000.html)
- Harmonoise: "two separate 'turbulence' effects … controlled by the same 'turbulence strength' parameter C": coherence loss between direct and reflected sound, and scattering into shielded regions, which "also limits the reduction under negative sound speed gradients". "At 1 kHz and 1000 m, the effective limitation on barrier attenuation is 35 + 10 log(C)"; typical C = 5e-6, the author used 1e-5. **[EARLIER NOTE, FULL]** — [AAS 2012, "The Harmonoise noise prediction algorithm"](https://www.acoustics.asn.au/conference_proceedings/AAS2012/papers/p43.pdf)
- ISO 9613-2 caps barrier attenuation D_z at 20 dB (single edge) and 25 dB (multiple edges). That the caps stand in for turbulence and meteorology appears only in a search summary, not in a primary source. **[SNIPPET]** — [DataKustik CadnaA manual, "Industrie ISO"](https://doku.datakustik.com/CadnaA/en/Industrie_ISO.html)
- Engineering models for upwind shadows "typically impose a distance independent constant value for the sound pressure level relative to free field propagation". **[ABS]** — [Van Renterghem et al. 2023](https://consensus.app/papers/details/22efe341dcaf52d8a779d184086712c6/?utm_source=claude_desktop)
- FAA/Volpe exercise of Nord2000 for aircraft: "strong turbulence" changed results by 0.1–0.2 dB in the cases run, but a "proof of concept investigation into turbulence effects over longer range propagation where grazing angles of the ground-reflected ray are very shallow indicated that turbulence could have a significant effect". **[EARLIER NOTE, FULL text-searched]** — [Ahearn et al., DOT/FAA/AEE/2012-5](https://rosap.ntl.bts.gov/view/dot/9687/dot_9687_DS1.pdf)
- sonAIR (Empa) couples a detailed source model, helicopters included, with the sonX propagation model, with "particular attention to air attenuation through a stratified atmosphere" and a physics-based ground model. Empa's turbulence work (Lincke et al.) is on arrays and auralisation, "based on measurable meteorological parameters and … von Kármán turbulence spectra". Whether sonX applies a turbulence coherence factor in mapping is not stated in what I found. **[SNIPPET]** — [Empa sonAIR page](https://www.empa.ch/web/s509/sonair); [Lincke 2023](https://elib.dlr.de/194540/2/10.0016847.pdf) **[FULL]**
- Research PEs for elevated sources often omit turbulence and say the shadow is then wrong: CNPE against a wind turbine was off by "more than 20 dB at 845 m" upwind ([Könecke et al. 2023](https://wes.copernicus.org/articles/8/639/2023/)); shadow levels without turbulence are "unrealistically low" (Nyborg et al. 2023). **[EARLIER NOTE, FULL]**
- Representative strengths: μ0² = 3e-5 is "the upper limit of turbulence that one would normally encounter", and 1e-6 to 2e-6 fitted White & Gilbert's upwind data (Lam 2009, **[FULL]**). Daigle's shadow data had measured ⟨μ²⟩ = 6e-6, L = 1.6 m (McBride, **[EARLIER NOTE]**). Cotté used μ² = 1e-5, L0 = 5 m (**[FULL]**).

### Inferences
- **[RECALLED]** CNOSSOS-EU, AEDT/INM and ANOPP carry no explicit turbulence parameter. AEDT's lateral attenuation (SAE AIR-5662) is an empirical fit to measured flyovers, so it implicitly includes the average turbulence and ground state of its data set; `aircraft_low_elevation_practice.md` covers these. Not re-verified here.
- Nord2000's "temperature-gradient fluctuation" input is the engineering form of the large-scale, frequency-independent mechanism in section 1. A recommended default of zero means that, out of the box, Nord2000 also predicts a sharp shadow boundary.
- Converting units matters: Lam's and Cotté's μ² is a Gaussian or von Kármán *variance* of the index, while γ_T and C² are *structure parameters* (per m^(2/3)). They are not interchangeable without the outer scale, and NICEOPS's `C² = γ_T/4` is the structure-parameter form **[PROJECT]**.
- The defaults (γ_T ≈ 5e-6 to 8e-6) correspond to neutral u* ≈ 0.3 m/s at ~10 m (section 2 table). A calm desert morning should be lower aloft, and a desert midday 2–10× higher near the ground.

### Gaps
- Nord2000's formulas for its coherence factor and "scattering zone" level (AV 1851/00), and how C_v², C_T² enter: inaccessible (FORCE links blocked).
- HAR29TR-041118's scattering formula with its frequency, range and height terms: not seen. Only the secondary "35 + 10 log C at 1 kHz, 1 km" is in hand.
- No source gives γ_T by Pasquill class or day/night category for Harmonoise or Imagine; any such table found later should be checked against the MOST estimates above.

## 5. Measured shadow-zone levels and the "shadow floor"

### Takeaway
Measured upwind and refractive-shadow excess attenuations top out around 20–30 dB below the downwind or free-field level. Above a few hundred hertz they depend weakly on range, height and frequency, and they rise with heat flux and fall with stronger upward refraction. For elevated sources the measured shadow is much shallower, a couple of dB(A) at ~1 km for a wind turbine. That agrees with the model claim that turbulence (and shadow geometry) matter less for elevated sources until well past the boundary.

### Cited Findings
- Wiener & Keast: over open level terrain the upwind excess attenuation exceeded the downwind one "by as much as 25–30 dB for source and receiver heights of 12 and 5 ft", with fluctuation statistics correlated with the micrometeorology. **[SNIPPET]** — [Wiener & Keast, JASA 31 (1959)](https://labs.sonicfield.org/library/some-experimental-results-concerning-the-propagation-of-sound-over-ground)
- 250–4000 Hz to 250 m: below the refractive shadow boundary the levels follow linear-gradient diffraction theory, "however, at the largest distances and highest frequencies, there was evidence of additional energy penetrating the refractive shadow from scattering by atmospheric turbulence". **[ABS]** — [Embleton et al., JASA (1986)](https://consensus.app/papers/details/4106f839b78c55b1a7d264dc76858f34/?utm_source=claude_desktop)
- Diffraction dominates at low frequency and scattering at high: "in the short wavelength limit … scattering due to turbulence should be the predominant mechanism". **[ABS]** — ["A refractive shadow zone", NASA N91-16703](https://consensus.app/papers/details/2713976d6519501380ef2ad8ded123a0/?utm_source=claude_desktop)
- Modeled shadow floors with turbulence: about −20 dB re free field at 100 Hz–1 kHz to 10 km at μ0² = 3e-5 (Lam 2009 **[FULL]**). In Cotté's 400 Hz, 1 m case the shadow level "remains relatively constant" beyond the boundary at ~150 m (Cotté 2007 **[FULL]**).
- Elevated source, seven months at 950 m from a wind farm in northern Sweden: "on average, 1.9 dBA lower sound levels are measured when the part of the rotor disk of the closest turbine is in the refractive shadow zone" (ray tracing on forecast meteorology), with more amplitude modulation when about half the rotor is shadowed. **[ABS]** — [Bolin et al., JASA (2020)](https://consensus.app/papers/details/232d0c794b5951f9b27581a400e65aa5/?utm_source=claude_desktop)
- Upwind, elevated sources, neutral: nonlinear profiles give caustic increases "of up to 7 dB" at intermediate ranges "potentially extending to a few kilometers" before the shadow, "motivated by experimental observations that indicate increases in SPL under upwind conditions". **[ABS]** — [Gharbi et al., Acta Acustica (2026)](https://consensus.app/papers/details/89f6fa0ca10555559ddad6abe66d18f4/?utm_source=claude_desktop)
- NICEOPS day 284 (45 m source): the still-air PE puts the shadow onset within ~50 m of the measured one in every band 250 Hz–2 kHz; a turbulent PE at γ_T 3e-6 gives −17 to −18 dB against −12 to −17 dB measured, while still air falls below −35 dB; γ_T 3e-5 fills the shadows to −3 dB re free field **[PROJECT]**.

### Inferences
- The NICEOPS day-284 shadow depth (−12 to −17 dB re free field) is consistent with the literature's −20 to −30 dB upwind floors for near-ground sources: shallower, as expected for an elevated source with near-grazing paths. It is also consistent with Bolin's small measured shadow effect.
- The field record supports Harmonoise's design, where the strength that limits shadows also sets coherence loss, only for near-ground geometries. For elevated sources and flush receivers the two effects separate (no coherence loss at a plate; scattering still active), which NICEOPS already reflects.

### Gaps
- Plateau values in dB re free field by frequency band from the classic campaigns (Wiener & Keast's figures, Havelock et al., Daigle's IOA paper, L'Espérance's data) could not be opened.
- No measured shadow-floor data were found for helicopters or other aircraft at 0–10° elevation and 1–4 km.
- Recent UAV campaigns were not located beyond the NWTC tower experiment, which uses a ground source.

## 6. Is "a flat-in-frequency residual cannot be scattering" defensible?

### Takeaway
Only in part. It holds for the mechanism it was probably aimed at, direct/reflected coherence loss at a 1.2 m microphone, which scales as k², and for any flush receiver in a lit region, where turbulence leaves the energy mean unchanged. It does not hold as a general statement. In a shadow, small-scale scattering is near-flat above ~200–300 Hz (NICEOPS's own flush fit is −1.55 dB per decade), and large-scale fluctuation of the refraction moves the shadow boundary by a frequency-independent amount. Whether the far-ahead 2–6° residual could be turbulence therefore turns on one checkable fact: whether those receivers are lit with margin, or near or inside a predicted shadow or caustic, in each band.

### Cited Findings
- Weak frequency dependence of shadow levels above a few hundred hertz, measured and explained by Kolmogorov scattering: [Di et al. 1995](https://consensus.app/papers/details/fe8b084dd4415b7ea95ebe41ab2217bb/?utm_source=claude_desktop); [Gilbert et al. 1996](https://consensus.app/papers/details/691dead398905913b3139ff366ee7368/?utm_source=claude_desktop); Gilbert, Raspet & Di 1990 **[ABS]**.
- Turbulence negligible at 40 and 90 Hz in a shadow (Di 1995 **[ABS]**). Diffraction and scattering are equal at 250 Hz in Daigle's 230 m data (McBride **[EARLIER NOTE]**).
- Ray displacement by turbulence is frequency-independent (Lam 2009, Eq. 19 **[FULL]**). Upwind prediction errors are attributed to "the variable position of the shadow-zone boundary" (Wilson et al. 2015 **[FULL]**).
- Mean-profile predictions are within ~2 dB rms outside shadows and minima (Wilson et al. 2015 **[FULL]**). Turbulence is negligible to 800 m for an elevated source (Kayser 2023 **[ABS]**), and smaller for elevated sources generally (Johnson, Raspet & Bobak 1987 **[SNIPPET]**).
- NICEOPS fits: flush S = 39.18 + 10 lg γ_T − 1.55 lg(f/1 kHz) + 2.10 lg(R/100 m) + 5.30 lg(z_s/100 m); 1.2 m: 36.67, +5.61, 5.13, 5.30 **[PROJECT]**.

### Inferences
- **Mechanism by mechanism, expected signature across 63–630 Hz:**

  | mechanism | flush (plate) | 1.2 m pole | spectral shape | sign |
  | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
  | coherence loss, direct/reflected | none (γ_p = 0) | ∝ k²; 0 → −2.6 dB at maxima, more at dips (section 1 table) | strongly rising with f | − at maxima, + at dips |
  | backscatter extinction | ≤ 2e-3 dB | same | ∝ k^(1/3) | − |
  | lit-region scintillation | energy conserved | energy conserved (apart from coherence) | n/a | 0 |
  | small-scale scattering into a deep shadow | yes | yes | ~flat above 200–300 Hz; negligible below ~100 Hz | + |
  | large-scale boundary wander / random refraction (penumbra, caustics) | yes | yes | frequency-independent in the ray limit | + just inside the boundary, − at caustic peaks |
  | frame-selection (censoring) bias in the measurement | yes | yes | flat if selection is broadband | + (measured louder) |

- For the **far-ahead, fast, high-flight** residual (prediction 1.5–4 dB too quiet, flat, on every line): if the predicted field there is lit with margin, turbulence cannot supply it at the plates, and the "source-side" conclusion stands against turbulence. If it lies within a diffraction length or two of a predicted shadow boundary, or at the far side of a caustic, then a frequency-flat turbulent contribution of this size is physically reasonable and the argument fails. A shallow-elevation boundary within 1–4 km is common under the daytime lapse a desert airfield develops, and for upwind lines under any wind. The deciding diagnostic is how far each residual point sits from NICEOPS's own predicted boundary (`ShadowTransition`'s ξ = (r − r_b)/F) for that frame. The residual should be binned by ξ: a turbulence or boundary-wander origin predicts a residual that grows as ξ → 0⁺ and is roughly constant deeper in; a source origin predicts no dependence on ξ.
- "On every microphone line" is weak evidence against wind-driven shadows, which are directional. It is not evidence against a temperature-lapse shadow, which is azimuthally uniform, nor against censoring bias, which is geometric in SNR.
- For the **low, slow sources near the array, 3–4 dB too loud**: turbulence can only *lower* a lit energy mean by decorrelating a constructive interference (1.2 m only, and small at short range below 630 Hz) or by smearing a caustic. Neither acts at plates or flat in frequency at short range. Turbulence is not a plausible explanation there.
- **The project's own fits contradict the blanket claim.** NICEOPS's flush-microphone scattering law is flat to 1.6 dB across 63–630 Hz. A "flat → not scattering" argument is therefore inconsistent with NICEOPS's own PE wherever the scattering term would be active. It is consistent only where the scattering term is inactive, which is lit receivers.
- A cheap test inside NICEOPS: rerun the far-ahead frames with `--scattering` at γ_T = 3e-6 and with a Nord2000-style boundary wander (an ensemble of linear-fit gradients, σ_a set from the lidar's 10 min variance, energy-averaged). If either closes the 1.5–4 dB without moving the lit, near-array frames, the residual is propagation. If neither moves those frames, they are lit and the source-side reading stands.

### Gaps
- Whether the far-ahead residual frames are lit or near a boundary in NICEOPS's predictions was not checked: that is data-side work, outside this literature review.
- How the harness selects 0.5 s frames (any SNR or level threshold) was not checked; it determines whether the censoring bias applies.
- No published quantification of the boundary-wander energy gain versus σ of the sound-speed gradient was found; it would have to be computed.
