# Wave-based, energy-based and analytical methods for propagation into urban street canyons and shielded courtyards

Scope note for the report writer: almost the entire literature found here concerns a **ground-level source in one canyon and a receiver in another** (road traffic, "quiet side"). Work with an elevated source is limited to two kinds: aircraft-noise measurements in courtyards (Lugten, Schiphol) and the sonic-boom FDTD studies (Dragna, Emmanuelli). Statements about what this means for an elevated helicopter source are inferences and are marked as such. Several sources could be retrieved only as abstracts. The full texts read were Van Renterghem et al. 2006, Wei et al. 2014, Kang et al. (Sheffield conference paper) and Richoux et al. 2009, so their numbers are the most reliable.

## 1. Full-wave references (FDTD, PSTD, BEM, ESM, TLM, FEM, DG, 3-D PE): cost and frequency limits

### Takeaway
In this literature, the full-wave reference for canyons is almost always **2-D or 2.5-D** (FDTD, Fourier PSTD, the equivalent sources method (ESM) or BEM). A full 3-D wave solution is avoided because of its cost, even by the groups who fit engineering models to it. Published 2-D canyon runs stop at about 1.25-1.4 kHz with roughly 10 cells per wavelength. Above about 1-2 kHz the practical references are energy and geometric methods.

### Cited Findings
- **FDTD canyon (2-D), concrete parameters.** Van Renterghem et al. 2006 used a 2-D FDTD domain 4 m wide (7 < x < 11 m) and 51 m high, with a 0.025 m grid ("about 10 computational cells per wavelength" at 1405 Hz, the top of the 1250 Hz band), dt = 5.19 µs (2-D CFL ≈ 1), 40-cell PMLs tuned for 120 dB attenuation at normal incidence, and a broadband Gaussian pulse (730 Hz centre, 500 Hz −3 dB bandwidth). Levels stopped changing after 15,000 time steps. The band was 50-1250 Hz. Atmospheric absorption was omitted as negligible over those distances and frequencies. — [Van Renterghem, Salomons & Botteldooren 2006, Appl. Acoust. 67:487-510](https://users.ugent.be/~tvrenter/publicaties/streetcanyonsFDTDPE.pdf)
- FDTD is described there as the method that can handle complex geometry, (inhomogeneous) absorbing and partly diffusely reflecting surfaces, and a moving, inhomogeneous, turbulent atmosphere at the same time. Moving-medium runs used a Fluent CFD flow field, a staggered-in-space grid and the "prediction-step staggered-in-time" (PSIT) scheme. Facade impedance was modeled as locally reacting (real, frequency-independent normalized Z = 5, 10 or rigid). — [Van Renterghem et al. 2006](https://users.ugent.be/~tvrenter/publicaties/streetcanyonsFDTDPE.pdf)
- **Even engineering-model calibration avoids 3-D.** The Wei et al. (2014) engineering model was fitted to "an extensive set of full-wave numerical calculations" and states that "the full wave numerical model used to extract the coefficients … cannot be used for 3D simulations due to computational cost". It therefore uses a 2.5-D approach: 3-D is approximated by summing many 2-D sections, with facades "twisted" normal to each source-receiver line, plus a 3-D free-field spreading correction. The authors state the twisting error is "reasonably small". The same approach underlies Harmonoise and CNOSSOS-EU. — [Wei, Botteldooren, Van Renterghem, Hornikx, Forssén, Salomons & Ögren 2014, Acta Acust. united Ac. 100(6):1098-1111](https://publications.lib.chalmers.se/records/fulltext/206833/local_206833.pdf)
- **2.5-D ESM.** For a geometry invariant in one direction, an inverse Fourier transform of 2-D Helmholtz solutions gives the 3-D field for arbitrary source and observer positions. This "2.5-dimensional" method (Duhamel 1996) was extended to parallel urban canyons using ESM for the 2-D solutions. Multiple canyon reflections demand a **very fine frequency resolution** in the 2-D solutions to keep the transform error small. An incoherent line source is obtained "much more efficiently" than from a 3-D solution. Using a coherent line source (a pure 2-D result) for shielded-canyon receivers "leads mostly to an overprediction of levels" and can give wrong conclusions about noise abatement. No atmospheric effects are included. — [Hornikx & Forssén 2007, JASA 122(5):2532](https://doi.org/10.1121/1.2783197)
- **Fourier PSTD, 3-D courtyards.** Hornikx & Forssén modeled propagation into 3-D urban courtyards with the "extended Fourier PSTD" method (Appl. Acoust. 2011). Its abstract and numerical results could not be retrieved. — [Hornikx & Forssén 2011, Appl. Acoust.](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.apacoust.2011.03.005)
- **openPSTD** is the open-source Fourier PSTD implementation "geared towards applications in the built environment". It is licensed **GPL v3**. It models geometry as a composition of rectangular 2-D subdomains, so it was initially limited to orthogonal 2-D situations. Run times are given as "minutes up to hours" depending on dimension. Developers: Hornikx, Krijnen, van Harten. — [openPSTD GitHub](https://github.com/openPSTD/openPSTD); [Research Software Directory](https://research-software-directory.org/software/openpstd); [Hornikx et al., Comput. Phys. Commun. 2016](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0010465516300443)
- **Hybrid PSTD/DG (TU/e).** Fourier PSTD struggles with complex boundaries (buildings, topography, frequency-dependent impedance), and nodal DG becomes prohibitively expensive at large scale. The two are coupled through a buffer zone, using DG near boundaries and PSTD in the bulk. The method was later extended from the effective-sound-speed approximation to the full wind vector in the linearized Euler equations (LEE). — [Pagán Muñoz & Hornikx 2017, J. Comput. Phys.](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jcp.2017.07.046); [Cosnefroy & Hornikx 2021, TU/e](https://research.tue.nl/en/publications/23f8c2b2-509b-43c5-ba9a-ebed02f0a051)
- **BEM.** Salomons et al. 2009 built their engineering algorithm for multiple canyon reflections on "numerical boundary-element calculations of canyon-to-canyon propagation". — [Salomons, Polinder, Lohman, Zhou, Borst & Miedema 2009, JASA 126(5):2340](https://doi.org/10.1121/1.3238238)
- **ESM with turbulence.** Ögren & Forssén extended ESM to include facade diffusion, absorption and atmospheric turbulence for canyon problems. — [Ögren 2004, Chalmers thesis (OpenAlex record)](https://api.openalex.org/works?search=Prediction%20of%20Traffic%20Noise%20Shielding%20by%20City%20Street%20Canyons); Ögren & Forssén, Appl. Acoust. 65:629-642 (2004), cited in [Wei et al. 2014](https://publications.lib.chalmers.se/records/fulltext/206833/local_206833.pdf)
- **3-D PE in a canyon.** A 3-D parabolic equation for a U-shaped street canyon with reflecting facades has been built: effective sound speed for temperature and wind, a Gaussian starter, a Padé propagator and an alternating-direction solver. It reproduces the canyon's modal behavior and was compared with experiment. — [Lihoreau & Richoux 2023, IOA proceedings](https://www.ioa.org.uk/system/files/proceedings/b_lihoreau_o_richoux_a_3d_parabolic_equation_code_for_street_canyon_propagation.pdf)
- **TLM.** The Gustave Eiffel/Cerema group (Guillaume, Écotière, Gauvreau) uses the transmission line matrix method for complex outdoor media with many reflections plus ground effect, for example a 3-D forest. Only qualitative results were found. — [Goestchel et al., Forum Acusticum 2023](https://doi.org/10.61782/fa.2023.0996)
- **Sonic boom, 2-D Euler FDTD.** Emmanuelli, Dragna, Ollivier and Blanc-Benon solve the full 2-D Euler equations with high-order finite-difference time-domain techniques. Their configurations are an isolated building, two buildings, multiple buildings and an urban geometry representative of European city centres. — [Dragna et al. 2022, conference paper](https://doi.org/10.25144/14380); [Emmanuelli et al. 2022, JASA 151(6):3792](https://hal.science/hal-03853864/file/dragna_jasa22.pdf); [Dragna et al. 2022, JASA 152(6):3323](https://pubs.aip.org/asa/jasa/article/152/6/3323/2839538)
- **Hornikx's review** ("Ten questions concerning computational urban acoustics", 2016) frames wave-based methods as mature in the preceding decade and appropriate for complex environments and research, with engineering methods kept for fast analysis and mapping. Only the abstract was retrieved; its cost figures were not. — [Hornikx 2016, Building and Environment](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.buildenv.2016.06.028)

### Inferences
- **Scaling, from the Van Renterghem grid** (dx = λ_min/10, CFL ≈ 1). Cost scales as f³ in 2-D and f⁴ in 3-D. Extrapolated from that paper's parameters:
  - **3-D FDTD at 1 kHz**: a 30 × 30 × 30 m canyon box at dx ≈ 34 mm is about 7×10⁸ cells, over several thousand steps. That is feasible on a GPU or cluster for a handful of reference cases.
  - **3-D FDTD at 10 kHz**: 10⁴ times that. Not feasible.
  - **2-D at 2 kHz**: a 30 × 30 m section is about 3×10⁶ cells, which is trivial.

  So 3-D wave references are realistic to about 1-2 kHz. The 2-4 kHz bands that dominate A-weighted helicopter levels need 2.5-D/2-D wave methods or energy/geometric methods.
- Fourier PSTD needs about 2 points per wavelength against FDTD's about 10. That is why the Chalmers/TU/e group chose it for 3-D courtyards; the requirement is consistent with openPSTD's design. Its weakness is complex boundaries, which the PSTD/DG hybrid fixes. This is general knowledge consistent with the cited descriptions; the points-per-wavelength figures are not quoted from a retrieved source.
- For NICE-OPS, the cheapest high-fidelity *offline* reference is a 2-D (or 2.5-D via Duhamel's transform) FDTD, PSTD or BEM solve of a canyon cross-section under **plane-wave incidence**. A source 50-1500 m away is effectively a plane wave across a 10-30 m canyon. The solve should be tabulated against elevation angle, azimuth relative to the street axis, band, H/W, receiver position and facade impedance.

### Gaps
- No retrieved source gives wall-clock or CPU-hour costs for a 3-D canyon FDTD, PSTD, BEM or FEM case at a stated frequency. The "~8000 CPU-h" figure for Dragna et al. 2022 in the task brief was **not verified**: the papers could not be fetched (HAL returned bot protection, AIP returned 403), and the press release gives no cost.
- FEM applied to street canyons: no source found.
- Retrieved TLM work is for forests, not canyons. No quantitative TLM urban validation was found.
- Hornikx & Forssén 2011 (3-D courtyards, PSTD): no numbers retrieved, such as 3-D versus 2-D level differences or run times.

## 2. Hybrid methods (PE-FDTD, PE-PSTD/DG, FDTD-diffusion, sonic boom)

### Takeaway
The canonical hybrid is Van Renterghem & Botteldooren's **one-way FDTD → GF-PE coupling**. FDTD resolves the reverberant canyon, and a vertical array of time signals recorded near the canyon edge is Fourier-transformed into a PE starting field. With a symmetry/reciprocity doubling trick it matched full FDTD "very well". It does not extend to backscatter or arbitrary geometry. Other hybrids couple a wave model to a diffusion-energy model (Pasareanu), or PSTD to DG.

### Cited Findings
- **The coupling.** FDTD runs in the complex source region (the canyon) and PE above the roofs, where one-way propagation holds and the wind is mainly horizontal. A single broadband FDTD run fills the source canyon. "At a short distance from the canyon edge, time signals are recorded on a vertical array to generate starting functions for the PE method, using a transition from the time domain to the frequency domain by means of FFT." GF-PE then runs per frequency. In the 2006 study the starting field sits 4 m past the edge of the middle building. — [Van Renterghem et al. 2006](https://users.ugent.be/~tvrenter/publicaties/streetcanyonsFDTDPE.pdf); coupling details in [Van Renterghem, Salomons & Botteldooren 2005, Acta Acust. united Ac. 91:671-679] (cited therein, not retrieved)
- **PE grid.** 20 frequencies per third-octave band, vertical step λ/10, horizontal step 5λ, and 4096 vertical points including a 1500-point absorbing layer. — [Van Renterghem et al. 2006](https://users.ugent.be/~tvrenter/publicaties/streetcanyonsFDTDPE.pdf)
- **The symmetry trick.** For identical source and receiver canyons with mirrored positions, the transfer function a→b is approximately twice the transfer function a→c, where c is an intermediate receiver 2 m above the roof on the symmetry plane. The argument: a→c involves one edge diffraction and a→b two, and c→b equals a→c by symmetry and reciprocity. Because the rigid-roof reflection is then counted twice, **6 dB is subtracted**. It agreed "very well" with full FDTD from source to receiver. Asymmetric cases fall back to full FDTD. — [Van Renterghem et al. 2006](https://users.ugent.be/~tvrenter/publicaties/streetcanyonsFDTDPE.pdf)
- The authors describe the FDTD-PE model's general applicability in urban areas as "limited". It is "well suited and numerically efficient in prototype situations", which they used for parameter studies. — [Van Renterghem et al. 2006](https://users.ugent.be/~tvrenter/publicaties/streetcanyonsFDTDPE.pdf)
- **PE plus ESM over intermediate canyons.** Schiff, Hornikx & Forssén used the PE and ESM to characterize the insertion loss of intermediate canyons between source and receiver. The result can then be applied "without the overhead of a detailed numerical calculation". The study varied canyon/rooftop absorption, variable rooftop height, multiple canyons and correlated versus uncorrelated sources. — [Schiff, Hornikx & Forssén 2008, POMA](https://doi.org/10.1121/1.2988049); [Schiff et al. 2010, Appl. Acoust.](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.apacoust.2010.01.005)
- **FDTD + diffusion.** Pasareanu et al. use a full-wave model (FDTD) near the source, where interference matters, and outside the cluttered region, where conditions are free-field-like. A diffusion model covers regions where diffusion conditions hold. They highlight diffraction near building edges as what pure diffusion models get wrong. — [Pasareanu, Burdisso & Remillieux 2018, JASA](https://doi.org/10.1121/1.5027506); [Pasareanu 2014, Virginia Tech dissertation](http://hdl.handle.net/10919/47601)
- **PSTD/DG.** Coupled through a buffer zone, with DG near geometric boundaries and Fourier PSTD in the bulk; extended to the full wind vector in the LEE. — [Cosnefroy & Hornikx 2021](https://research.tue.nl/en/publications/23f8c2b2-509b-43c5-ba9a-ebed02f0a051)
- **Sonic boom (2-D Euler FDTD, an elevated source and so the closest analogue to NICE-OPS).**
  - Isolated building: an illuminated zone with amplification in front and a large reduction in the shadow behind, with levels following a geometric analysis closely.
  - Street canyons: waveforms show extra facade and street reflections plus **low-frequency oscillations from local canyon modes**, governed by the aspect ratio H/W.
  - Wider streets relative to building height are less affected. Multiple buildings "don't affect boom loudness" but prolong ground-level signals.

  — [Dragna et al. 2022, conference paper](https://doi.org/10.25144/14380); [EurekAlert summary of Emmanuelli et al. 2022](https://www.eurekalert.org/news-releases/955021); [Dragna et al. 2022, JASA 152:3323 (abstract via search)](https://pubs.aip.org/asa/jasa/article/152/6/3323/2839538)
- **Terrain (same group, 2-D Euler FDTD, profiles longer than 10 km).** Mild slopes change the noise level by less than 1 dB. Steep slopes give +3 dB at 1% of ground positions and up to 5-6 dB near depressions, from wavefront folding. — [Emmanuelli et al. 2023, JASA](https://doi.org/10.1121/10.0019938)

### Inferences
- For an elevated source the natural coupling runs in **reverse** of Van Renterghem's. NICE-OPS's long-range model (straight or curved ray, or its split-step Padé PE) supplies the incident field at the canyon top. A local canyon solver, or a precomputed canyon transfer table, then carries it to the receiver. By reciprocity the two directions are equivalent: putting the source at the canyon receiver and recording on a vertical line above the roofs gives Van Renterghem's configuration exactly. NICE-OPS's PE could then carry the field from the canyon top toward the aircraft along each bearing.
- For NICE-OPS's pair count (10⁸-10⁹), a direct hybrid per pair is out of the question. The hybrid belongs **offline**: tabulate a canyon insertion loss (re free field, per band) indexed by (elevation, relative azimuth, H/W, receiver depth and offset, facade class). Wei et al. (Section 3) took the same route for traffic noise, fitting closed forms to about 20,000 FDTD runs.

### Gaps
- No quantitative error (dB) of FDTD-PE against full FDTD was retrievable beyond "very good agreement". The figures could not be read from the extracted text.
- Hornikx's PE-PSTD coupling was not retrieved.
- Wall-clock cost and per-band error of the sonic-boom urban runs were not retrievable.

## 3. Energy and analytic canyon models (radiosity, diffusion, transport, reverberant-tail formulae, modal theories)

### Takeaway
Energy models (Kang's radiosity, Picaut's diffusion equation, Le Pollès's transport theory) reproduce scale-model and full-scale **reverberation time and level decay along a street**. Simple regression formulae track the full models to within about 2 dB in level. They describe the **reverberant field inside a canyon**, not diffraction into a shielded canyon. The best-calibrated shielded-side engineering model (Wei et al. 2014: an image-source-derived closed form fitted to FDTD) has a **2.7 dB standard deviation** against the full-wave results.

### Cited Findings
- **Radiosity (Kang).** Boundaries are divided into patches and the model computes energy exchange between them, giving impulse responses and so RT, EDT and SPL. Parametric ranges for squares: L and W 20-200 m, H 5-100 m, facade absorption 0.1-0.9.
  - The derived RT formula matches the radiosity model to within 10-15%.
  - The SPL formula L = L_W + 10 log(Q/(4πr²) + (3H/(W+L))·4/R) matches it "generally within 2 dB", but is "inapplicable to long rectangular spaces, where the SPL attenuation is considerably greater".
  - Eyring's formula becomes increasingly inaccurate as side/height ratio and absorption increase.

  — [Kang, Meng & Brown, "Sound propagation in micro-scale urban areas" (Sheffield)](https://staffwww.dcs.shef.ac.uk/people/G.Brown/pdf/SS25-034-IP-Kang-UK.pdf)
- Diffusely reflecting boundaries give "significantly higher" level attenuation along a street than geometrically (specularly) reflecting ones. The radiosity model was validated against full-scale data from five streets with different scattering conditions, with "good agreement" for SPL and RT (no dB figure in the snippet). — [Kang 2000, JASA 107:1394, via ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/12577078_Sound_propagation_in_street_canyons_Comparison_between_diffusely_and_geometrically_reflecting_boundaries)
- **Diffusion equation (Picaut).** The room-acoustic diffusion model was applied to streets without significant modification: a 3-D diffusion equation for energy density, a diffusion coefficient for the street's degree of diffusion, and an exchange coefficient for wall absorption. "When the diffusion coefficient is chosen correctly", predicted RT and attenuation agree with scale-model data. — [Picaut & Simon 1999, JASA](https://doi.org/10.1121/1.426126); [Picaut, Simon & Hardy 1999, JASA](https://doi.org/10.1121/1.428093)
- **Transport theory.** Le Pollès, Picaut & Bérengier model a street with partially diffusely reflecting boundaries. — [Le Pollès et al. 2004, JASA 116:2969](https://pubs.aip.org/asa/jasa/article-abstract/116/5/2969/541717)
- **Reverberation-based regression (Thomas et al.).** A simple regression on typical street width and facade height or roughness, from mobile measurements in 99 Ghent streets. The reflection ratio (RR) measures the energy in the reverberant part. It was validated by detailed measurements in a canyon at several source-receiver distances. — [Thomas, Van Renterghem, De Boeck, Dragonetti & Botteldooren 2013, JASA](https://doi.org/10.1121/1.4802641)
- **H/W in a ray/image hybrid (Lee & Kang).** SPL depends on H/W only for narrow streets. T30 and EDT grow with H/W. T30 falls with scattering coefficient, and EDT rises with scattering at H/W = 3 and 6. — [Lee & Kang 2014, Acta Acust. united Ac.](https://doi.org/10.3813/aaa.918806)
- **Shielded-side engineering model (Wei et al. 2014).**
  - Structure: L = L_W − A_diffr − A_free − A_inter + L_scatter, with A_diffr built from A_bar (direct path over the barrier) and A_can (paths with at least one canyon reflection, which "quickly dominates" A_bar).
  - A_can,flat is derived from image-source theory with three terms: the source canyon's reverberant field diffracted into the receiver canyon, the reverse, and the cross term. Fitting coefficients were F(0..3) = 1.04, 12.53, 21.75, 0.05, with facade reflection coefficient ρ = 0.97.
  - The fit covers **20,740 numerical (FDTD) results** with a **standard deviation of 2.7 dB**. It "cannot capture the increase when H_s > H_i" (source-canyon building taller than the intermediate one).
  - Roof shape: A_bar,roof = 0.27·A_bar,flat − 2.9, fitted on 1788 runs with 3.0 dB mean squared error. A_can,roof = −5 dB with both canyons present, −2.5 dB with one.
  - Intermediate canyons: about 1 dB per 100 m, capped at 5 dB (simulations give up to 10 dB without refraction). Varying intermediate building heights changes it by about 1.1 dB on average.
  - Typical Ghent geometry: canyon width 12 m, building width 24 m, mean height 10.9 m.

  — [Wei et al. 2014](https://publications.lib.chalmers.se/records/fulltext/206833/local_206833.pdf)
- **Flat city model (Ögren).** Treats all sources and receivers as lying on a flat rigid plane, plus a measured building-shielding correction that stays "within a relatively small range (6-10 dB)" across the areas studied. Courtyard levels measured "relatively constant" across a city area. — [Ögren 2004 (OpenAlex)](https://api.openalex.org/works?search=Prediction%20of%20Traffic%20Noise%20Shielding%20by%20City%20Street%20Canyons)
- **BEM-based engineering algorithm (Salomons).** Supported by a room-acoustic analysis of the reverberant source and receiver canyon fields. Applied to Amsterdam, it differs considerably from the standard engineering model for shielded buildings at Lden 40-60 dB. — [Salomons et al. 2009](https://doi.org/10.1121/1.3238238)
- **Analytic modal canyon models.** Richoux et al. combine a modal decomposition of the horizontal-plane field with a 2-D image-source model for attenuation along the street. Open-roof losses enter as a frequency-dependent coefficient. The model agrees with a 1:25 scale model and 2-D FDTD **up to about 100 Hz full scale** (a street 6.75 m high, 5 m wide, 75 m long). Above that, roof leakage "is not anymore uniformly distributed on all modes" and **leaky modes**, each with its own attenuation, are required. The early modal approach is Bullen & Fricke (J. Sound Vib. 46:33-42, 1976). — [Richoux, Ayrault, Pelat, Félix & Lihoreau 2009 (arXiv:0907.0605)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0907.0605); [Pelat, Félix & Pagneux 2009, JASA 126(6):2864 (leaky modes)](https://pubs.aip.org/asa/jasa/article-abstract/126/6/2864/627222)

### Inferences
- Energy models (radiosity, diffusion, the reverberant-tail formulae) are the natural production candidates for the **reverberant part inside a canyon**, once energy has entered it. They are cheap (closed form or a small linear system) and band-wise. What they lack is a model of **how energy enters a canyon from above at a given elevation angle**: the diffraction over the roof edge. In the traffic models that role is played by an FDTD-fitted term (A_can).
- A_can's 2.7 dB scatter against FDTD is a realistic floor for any closed-form shielded-side model calibrated this way. A per-geometry lookup table built from 2-D/2.5-D wave runs would be the step up.

### Gaps
- Heutschi's reverberant canyon model, the Davies and Thomas canyon models, and Pavic's analytic theory were not retrieved this session. Their formulae and accuracy remain unverified.
- No dB-level accuracy of radiosity or diffusion against *full-scale* canyon measurements was found beyond "good agreement".
- One search summary said the diffusion model "fails when the gradient of the energy density approaches zero", but no primary source was attached, so the claim is not reported as a finding.

## 4. The shielded side: how large is the shielding, and what does it depend on?

### Takeaway
For ground-level traffic sources, shielded-canyon levels sit about **20-40 dB below free field** (from FDTD-PE). They are dominated by multiple facade reflections in the source and receiver canyons. Shielding is **very sensitive to facade reflection** (rigid against Z = 10 brick is about 20 dB), to **facade relief and diffusion** (about 10 dB from 500 Hz up) and to **wind** (downwind loses about 10 dB of it). It is **insensitive to W/H once W/H ≥ 1**. Measured aircraft-noise courtyard shielding is about **8-14 dB(A)**, depending on facade and roof shape.

### Cited Findings
- **Standard 2-D configuration** (Van Renterghem 2006): H = W = 10 m, D = 100 m between canyons, facade Z = 10, rigid street and roofs, source and receiver 1 m high and 4 m off-centre.
  - **W/H.** Narrow canyons (W/H = 0.5) shield strongly. Relative level rises with W/H and becomes "more or less constant" once W exceeds H. Two mechanisms compete: steeper first diffraction angles, against less spreading between the repeated reflections of a narrow canyon. Results scale linearly with geometry, so the same relative levels appear at scaled frequencies.
  - **Facade reflection.** Z = 5, Z = 10 and rigid (normal-incidence |R| = 0.67, 0.82, 1). Shielding with rigid facades is "very poor". Going from rigid to Z = 10 changes the result by **about 20 dB**, because of the many facade interactions. Large glazed areas are therefore bad for quiet sides.
  - **Facade relief.** Recesses, sills and a roughened wall add shielding that grows with frequency: **about 10 dB from about 500 Hz** against flat facades. The mechanism is an altered radiation pattern of the source canyon, with more energy scattered up and down. The authors note this scattering was modeled in 2-D only.
  - **Balconies** add substantial shielding, especially in the lowest bands. Inclined parapets add more in some bands.
  - **Wind.** Downwind at u* = 1 m/s reduces shielding by **about 10 dB** in the traffic-noise bands, mainly through refraction. The reduction grows with wind speed, frequency and distance between canyons (D = 50, 100, 200 m).
  - **Combined effects are not additive.** Rigid facades plus balconies are underpredicted if the separate effects are added, while diffusion and downwind refraction counteract each other.
  - **Coherent against incoherent line source.** A coherent 2-D source overstates shielding.

  — [Van Renterghem et al. 2006](https://users.ugent.be/~tvrenter/publicaties/streetcanyonsFDTDPE.pdf)
- **A coherent line source (pure 2-D) "mostly" overpredicts shielded-canyon levels** compared with the 2.5-D incoherent solution. — [Hornikx & Forssén 2007](https://doi.org/10.1121/1.2783197)
- **1:40 scale model of two parallel canyons.** In the shielded canyon, levels and decay times are "quite constant over the length of the canyon", unlike in the exposed canyon. The energy-time curve shows a rise time correlated with level. Closing the canyon into a courtyard **enlarges** the benefit of facade absorption or diffusion on both sides. **Vertical diffusion** (scattering up and down) gives lower shielded-side levels than horizontal diffusion. — [Hornikx & Forssén 2008, Acta Acust. united Ac.](https://doi.org/10.3813/aaa.918030)
- **Realistic shielded sites in Gothenburg (2.5-D ESM).** Realistic facade absorption gives a **5 dB(A)** reduction, and changing the shielded side's ground from asphalt to grass gives **3 dB(A)**, both largest for the lowest receivers. When the dominant source is in a street canyon, treating that canyon beats treating the shielded one for receivers above source height. — [Hornikx & Forssén 2006](https://doi.org/10.13140/2.1.2324.8963)
- **Measured against mapped.** A standard END noise map underpredicts shielded facades by **more than 14 dB(A)** at one inner-yard position in Ghent. The A_can multiple-reflection term fixes the **low-frequency** spectrum but not the high-frequency one, where turbulence scattering had to be added. The validation used 9 facades (3 in inner yards) with 90 days of data. — [Wei et al. 2014](https://publications.lib.chalmers.se/records/fulltext/206833/local_206833.pdf)
- **"Quiet side" definition used there**: the shielded facade is at least 20 dB(A) below the most exposed one. — [Wei et al. 2014](https://publications.lib.chalmers.se/records/fulltext/206833/local_206833.pdf)
- **Measured building-shielding correction** in the flat city model: 6-10 dB. — [Ögren 2004](https://api.openalex.org/works?search=Prediction%20of%20Traffic%20Noise%20Shielding%20by%20City%20Street%20Canyons)
- **Aircraft (elevated source) measurements in courtyards** at a full-scale container field lab near Schiphol: three courtyards, ten microphones on facades facing toward and away from the flights, matched with radar and meteorological data. Position-dependent differences are **8/9 dB(A) for straight facades, up to 14 dB(A)** for a courtyard with a slanted facade/roof and inset or overhang. Slanted roofs perpendicular to the flight direction deflect sound out of the courtyard. — [Lugten, IOA proceedings](https://www.ioa.org.uk/system/files/proceedings/m_lugten_assessing_the_influence_of_street_canyon_shape_on_aircraft_noise_results_from_measurements_in_courtyards_near_amsterdam_schiphol_airport.pdf) (8/9 dB(A) per search snippet); **conflicting figure**: the Inter-Noise 2022 abstract says "11 dB(A) for a courtyard with straight facades" — [Lugten 2022/23, Inter-Noise](https://doi.org/10.3397/in_2022_0976); journal version [Lugten, Wuite, Peng & Tenpierik 2024, Building and Environment](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.buildenv.2024.111400)
- Related simulations near airports used Pachyderm (geometric acoustics) and ENVI-met. Building orientation and slope relative to the source were the most decisive factors for courtyard noise. — [IBPSA Building Simulation 2023 paper](https://publications.ibpsa.org/proceedings/bs/2023/papers/bs2023_1385.pdf); [Integrating simulation and measurement… airport neighbourhoods](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/378859576_Integrating_simulation_and_measurement_techniques_to_model_outdoor_noise_and_heat_in_airport_neighbourhoods_with_varying_urban_geometries)

### Inferences
- **For an elevated source, "shielded" depends strongly on elevation angle.** For a receiver at 1.5 m on the centreline of an H = W = 10 m canyon, the direct line of sight across the street axis is lost below an elevation of atan(8.5/5) ≈ 60°. Along the street axis it is never lost. So a helicopter at 300 m is in line of sight only within about ±170 m horizontal distance when crossing the street perpendicularly, but along the whole street's direction. Shielding for an aircraft is therefore azimuth- and elevation-dependent. This differs from the traffic results above, where the source is permanently shielded. *(Inference, simple geometry.)*
- The traffic-noise magnitudes (20-40 dB re free field, about 20 dB facade sensitivity) are an **upper bound** for helicopter courtyard shielding. The measured aircraft figure (8-14 dB(A)) is much smaller, because an elevated source illuminates the canyon from above for part of each event and the integrated SEL is dominated by the unshielded part. That is consistent with Lugten's numbers.
- Facade absorption and diffusion matter far more in the shielded case than in the exposed case. A geometric model that keeps only a few specular orders will **underpredict** shielded-side levels (the END/Wei result) unless it carries a reverberant or diffuse tail.

### Gaps
- Van Renterghem's 2010 work on refraction into shielded canyons, and Thorsson's work, were not retrieved. The only wind quantification here is the 2006 parameter study.
- No wave-based study was found with a helicopter-like **elevated** source and a canyon or courtyard receiver, giving shielding against elevation angle. This appears to be a genuine literature gap, apart from the sonic-boom FDTD work and Lugten's measurements.
- Numeric relative-SPL curves against frequency (Figs. 6, 7, 17 and 18 in Van Renterghem 2006) could not be digitized from the text extract. Only the stated deltas are reported.

## 5. Turbulence scattering into canyons

### Takeaway
Turbulence scattering puts a **floor** under shielded-side levels. It is what fixes the high-frequency underprediction at inner yards (Wei et al.), and under upwind conditions it cancels the extra shielding refraction would otherwise give (Van Renterghem). An engineering model exists (Forssén et al. 2014): single scattering with Kolmogorov turbulence, von Kármán saturation, and a canyon correction.

### Cited Findings
- **Forssén, Hornikx, Botteldooren, Wei et al. 2014.**
  - Starts from energy-based single scattering by homogeneous, isotropic Kolmogorov turbulence, with saturation from the von Kármán model as a first-order multiple-scattering approximation.
  - Parameterized over the effective height of the shielding obstacle and its distances to source and receiver.
  - Adds single- or double-canyon corrections including air attenuation, facade absorption and Fresnel weighting of the multiple facade reflections.
  - Inputs are geometry, frequency, and the strengths of velocity and temperature turbulence.

  — [Forssén et al. 2014, Acta Acust. united Ac. 100(5):810-815](https://doi.org/10.3813/aaa.918760)
- At shielded inner-yard positions, the multiple-reflection term improves low frequencies only. "Adding a turbulent scattering contribution becomes therefore essential" at high frequencies. — [Wei et al. 2014](https://publications.lib.chalmers.se/records/fulltext/206833/local_206833.pdf)
- **Turbulence in FDTD-PE.** Turbulence was put into the PE part with an isotropic von Kármán spectrum and a 10 m correlation length, velocity turbulence only, with strengths from measurements. The authors note that isotropy is a simplification, since turbulence should be stronger near building edges.
  - Upwind: turbulence "has shown to be very important. Almost no shielding is observed compared to calculations in a non-moving (non-turbulent) atmosphere". The large extra shielding at u* = 1 m/s without turbulence "will not be observed in practice".
  - Downwind: turbulence increases the wind effect "some further", but refraction dominates.

  — [Van Renterghem et al. 2006](https://users.ugent.be/~tvrenter/publicaties/streetcanyonsFDTDPE.pdf)
- **ESM with turbulence** gave "small increases" of shielded-side level. — [Ögren 2004](https://api.openalex.org/works?search=Prediction%20of%20Traffic%20Noise%20Shielding%20by%20City%20Street%20Canyons)

### Inferences
- NICE-OPS already has a fitted shadow-zone scattering term (`ShadowScattering`, PE phase screens). The Forssén et al. 2014 parameterization is the published counterpart for a building-shielded receiver and has the same structure: single scattering plus saturation, then a canyon correction. It is a candidate for a "scattered floor" added in energy to the geometric or wave canyon level, consistent with how NICE-OPS already treats terrain shadow.
- For helicopter SEL the scattered floor matters mainly when the canyon receiver is out of line of sight *and* the source is distant (low elevation). These are low-level portions of the event and are likely outside the 10-dB-down window, so the SEL effect may be small. That is unverified.

### Gaps
- No dB magnitude of the scattered contribution against frequency or canyon geometry was retrieved from Forssén et al. 2014; the abstract only was available.
- No study found addresses turbulence scattering into a canyon from an *elevated* source.

## 6. Low-frequency behavior: where ray methods fail, and simple corrections

### Takeaway
Below a few hundred hertz at full scale, a canyon behaves as an **open waveguide with leaky modes**, and canyon resonances are visible even in sonic-boom waveforms. Image-source and ray models miss this. A modal-plus-image model agrees with experiment up to about **100 Hz full scale** for a 5 m wide, 6.75 m high street, and above that leaky modes with mode-dependent losses are needed. Rotor harmonics at 10-30 Hz (λ = 11-34 m) sit squarely in the modal and diffraction regime of typical canyons.

### Cited Findings
- Richoux et al.: 2-D FDTD with open-roof radiation losses, an analytic model (horizontal-plane modes plus 2-D image sources for attenuation along the street), and a 1:25 scale model (a 6.75 m × 5 m × 75 m street at full scale).
  - All three agree up to **f = 100 Hz full scale**.
  - Above that, the open-roof leakage is mode-dependent and leaky modes must be introduced.
  - The open-roof reflection coefficient's modulus falls as frequency rises, so fewer reflections come back off the open top.
  - At the start of the paper image sources and ray tracing are classed as high-frequency methods.

  — [Richoux et al. 2009](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0907.0605); [Pelat, Félix & Pagneux 2009, JASA 126:2864](https://pubs.aip.org/asa/jasa/article-abstract/126/6/2864/627222)
- **Sonic boom.** Canyon waveforms carry low-frequency oscillations "related to resonant modes of the canyon", governed by aspect ratio. Multiple reflections "prolong the pressure signals at ground level". — [Dragna et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.25144/14380); [EurekAlert](https://www.eurekalert.org/news-releases/955021)
- **Balconies** give their largest shielding gain "especially near very low 1/3 octave bands". Shielding against frequency differs from geometric expectation because of interference, and a coherent 2-D source shows deep destructive interference that third-octave averaging only partly smooths out. — [Van Renterghem et al. 2006](https://users.ugent.be/~tvrenter/publicaties/streetcanyonsFDTDPE.pdf)
- The A_can image-source-derived engineering term fixes the low-frequency spectrum at shielded positions in measurement. A **3 dB** subtraction was applied to near-facade measurements below 250 Hz for direct/reflected coherence. — [Wei et al. 2014](https://publications.lib.chalmers.se/records/fulltext/206833/local_206833.pdf)
- The 3-D PE in a U-shaped canyon reproduces the modal behavior and was compared with experiment. — [Lihoreau & Richoux 2023](https://www.ioa.org.uk/system/files/proceedings/b_lihoreau_o_richoux_a_3d_parabolic_equation_code_for_street_canyon_propagation.pdf)

### Inferences
- **Cross-canyon modes.** For W = 10-20 m the first cross-canyon modes fall at c/(2W) ≈ 8.5-17 Hz, so rotor harmonics at 10-30 Hz excite the first one to three modes. A per-band ray or image model is not trustworthy there. The 10-30 Hz problem is cheap for a wave solver (λ/10 = 1-3 m cells), so a 2-D or even 3-D wave solution of the canyon at the lowest bands is affordable offline, and arguably as a reference for every canyon class. Above roughly 200-500 Hz, energy and geometric methods with diffuse tails become adequate, and wave methods expensive.
- **A practical split for NICE-OPS** (inference):
  1. **Wave-based tables** (2-D/2.5-D FDTD, PSTD or BEM under plane-wave incidence) for bands up to about 500 Hz-1 kHz, tabulated by (elevation, relative azimuth, H/W, receiver position, facade class).
  2. **Image-source plus reverberant tail** (Kang/Thomas/Wei forms) or radiosity/diffusion above that.
  3. The **in-house PE** as the reference for the canyon-top incident field, particularly for low-elevation, refracted, long-range cases.
- Helicopter A-weighted SEL is usually dominated by 0.1-4 kHz broadband and BVI content, so the low-frequency modal errors matter mainly for unweighted or C-weighted, low-frequency or infrasound metrics, and for indoor or perception studies. *(Inference, not from a retrieved source.)*

### Gaps
- No source found gives a simple, validated *correction* term (in dB) for applying ray or image models below the canyon's first mode. Richoux et al.'s modal-plus-image model is the closest, and it is formulated for propagation along a street from a source inside it, not for incidence from above.
- No validated low-frequency canyon result for an *elevated* source was found, beyond the qualitative sonic-boom modes.
